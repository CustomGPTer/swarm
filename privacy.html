Privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026. This policy covers the Swarm app for Android (package uk.jj.swarm) and this website. Swarm is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Swarm has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer never receives your searches, your torrents or anything else from your phone.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics and no tracking in the app.
  • BitTorrent is a public network. Other people sharing the same torrent can see your internet address, as they can with every torrent app.

What stays on your phone

Your torrent list, your recent searches, your search sources and any keys you entered for them, your RSS feeds, your watchlist, your settings and the DHT index are kept in the app's private storage on your device. If Swarm ever closes unexpectedly, a short report of the fault is kept there too. It is shown under About and backup and goes nowhere unless you copy it yourself.

Downloaded files are saved to the Swarm folder inside your phone's Download folder or, if you have not allowed file access, inside the app's own storage. Swarm does not ask for access to your contacts, your location or your microphone.

Files and media

All files access is used to save and sort downloads, and to read a file or folder you pick when you make a torrent of your own. The Video and Music tabs list the video and audio files on your phone. None of this leaves the phone, and nothing is shared with other people except the torrents in your list.

The camera

The camera is used only while you scan a QR code. No picture is saved.

Backup files

A backup file is made only when you ask for one, and is saved where you choose. It holds your torrent list, sources, feeds, watchlist and settings, including any keys for your sources, so keep it somewhere private.

The BitTorrent network

Swarm is a BitTorrent app. To download a torrent, your phone connects directly to other people's devices and to the services that help them find each other. Nothing passes through the developer.

Every one of these receives your device's internet address, because that is how the internet delivers a reply. Anyone sharing a torrent can see the addresses of the others sharing it, and so can work out that your address took part.

A VPN replaces your address with the VPN's. Swarm does not include one. It warns you when none is on, and Settings has an option to stop all torrent traffic until one is. The encryption setting disguises torrent traffic from simple network filters. It does not hide your address or what you are sharing from the other people sharing it.

The two built-in search sources are the Internet Archive and Linux Tracker. Each handles what it receives under its own policy, as does any source or feed you add.

The DHT crawler

The DHT is the shared network torrent apps use to find each other. To build the index on the DHT tab, Swarm takes part in it as an ordinary member: it asks other members which torrents they know of, then asks people sharing those torrents for the name and file list. It downloads none of the content. The members it talks to receive your internet address, as above.

The index is kept on your phone and is never uploaded. Unless you change it, the crawler runs while Swarm is open and while the phone is charging on Wi-Fi. Settings can restrict it further, switch it off, cap the size of the index or clear it.

Each of these receives your device's internet address, as any website does, and handles it under its own privacy policy.

Web pages are shown using Android System WebView, the web component that Google builds into Android. As in every app that uses it, it can check pages against Google's Safe Browsing list of dangerous sites and, if you have agreed on your phone to share usage and diagnostics with Google, send Google diagnostic data. Swarm adds nothing to either.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Data on your phone is kept until you remove it. Deleting a torrent removes it from the list and, if you tick the box, deletes its files as well. Settings has a button to clear the DHT index.
  • Downloaded files stay until you delete them, in Swarm or in your phone's file manager.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Swarm deletes everything it holds. Files in Download/Swarm stay on your phone.
  • Swarm is left out of Android's backups, so nothing it holds is copied to your Google account.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Swarm holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. Your data is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. The developer has no control over what trackers, search sources or other people on the BitTorrent network record. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

The app's data is held in private storage that other apps cannot read. Swarm will not open program files that arrive in a torrent, and warns about results that look disguised or fake. Those checks are a help, not a guarantee: a file from a stranger can still be harmful.

Swarm connects over HTTPS wherever a source offers it. Many trackers, and some websites, offer only an unencrypted connection, and Swarm will still use them.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other websites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app file is downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

Swarm is a general-purpose file sharing app. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Changes

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed.

Contact

For a question about this policy or about your data, open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so please do not include personal details in one.
